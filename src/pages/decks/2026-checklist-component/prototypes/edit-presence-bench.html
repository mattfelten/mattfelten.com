Showing that someone is inside an item

The avatar hangs off the top-left corner, deliberately away from the author tag on the right. A row already carries one avatar; a second one beside it would read as a second author rather than as presence.

Highlight and dimmed say opposite things. A highlight means come and look; dimming means hands off. Whichever you pick should agree with the locking control below, because dimming a row you are still allowed to type in is a lie.

The avatar and the dim carry two different facts. The avatar is there for as long as somebody has the field focused. The row dims while their words are moving, and lifts a second and a half after their changes save, so it reads as "being changed" rather than literally "unsaved".

That tail matters. Autosave lands during the pauses people take to think of a word, and lifting the dim on each save would blink the row on and off over an ordinary typing rhythm. Typing again cancels the pending lift, so it never flickers.

Dimming also fights an earlier decision. The whole point of sending keystrokes live is that people can follow along; greying the text out is the one treatment that makes the words harder to read at exactly the moment they are changing.

What crosses the wire

Type on the left. Grey lines are ephemeral client events, dark lines are writes.

Why the avatar hangs off the corner. Inside the row it competes with the author tag and reads as co-authorship. Off the corner, overlapping the edge with a ring and a shadow, it reads as somebody leaning over the row rather than belonging to it, and it costs the text no space at all.

The case against locking. Two people editing the same sentence is rare; one person leaving a tab open on a row nobody else can touch is not. A hard hold turns a rare collision into a common obstruction, which is presumably why Slack shows who is in a row and then gets out of the way. If we do hold it, the timeout is not a nicety.
