Arbitration bench

Three gestures want the same pixels: dragging a row to reorder it, clicking its text to edit, and dragging across that text to select it. Try all three in every setting and watch the scorecard.

The one property the whole thing turns on

Reordering wants inline, so the ragged tail after the last line is not part of the text and stays grabbable. Editing wants block, so the click target is the whole rectangle including that tail. One element, one property, two features.

Pressing on the text and moving

Selection is not optional decoration: there is no toolbar, so the format bar appears on selection and is the only way to make anything bold.

What that gesture did

Drag a row, click some text, and drag across some text.

This round is not expected to come out clean. Three gestures, one region, and only one of them can own a plain press. The useful outcome is knowing exactly what each arrangement costs, so that when a rule arrives from outside the interaction design it settles the argument instead of adding to it.
